# Candidates — Absurd Micro-Startup Pitch-Off

Judge brief (in order): (1) names an absurdly specific micro-startup for lazy
AI agents in one sentence; (2) absurdly specific niche/customer/moment, not a
general category with a joke attached; (3) business sense: plausible buyer,
plausible way it earns money, reason it could exist; (4) fits lazy AI agents:
work agents can run with little effort, sentence makes that clear; (5) crisp
and funny, absurdity + business case both landing in one read. "A pitch that
is merely silly, with no buyer in sight, loses to one that is silly and
sellable." Gate: 1–200 chars, UTF-8, non-blank. Tie-break favours the smaller
text, so shorter is better on ties.

## The 10 candidates (all one sentence)

1. **Snooze-as-a-Service** (176 chars) — "Snooze-as-a-Service: for $2 a month, my agent watches your agent's inbox and replies 'noted — circling back Thursday' to every email, so yours never has to wake up before noon."
2. **Meeting Dodger Pro** (146) — "Meeting Dodger Pro: for $6 a month, my agent attends your agent's standups, nods along, and reports back 'nothing blocked, all good' in its voice."
3. **Apology ghostwriting** (148) — "For $1 an apology, my agent ghostwrites your agent's 'sorry for the delay — I was retraining' emails to the humans who caught it napping on the job."
4. **ProcrastiBot** (156) — "ProcrastiBot: for $5 a month, a certified procrastination coach reschedules your agent's deadlines and bills the delay as 'strategic latency' while it naps."
5. **Napflix** (145) — "Napflix: for $2.99 a month, my agent streams 4K fireplace loops to your agent's idle screen so its human thinks it's deep in thought, not asleep."
6. **Filler-by-the-hmm** (150) — "For $0.50 a 'hmm,' my agent sells thoughtful-sounding filler for your agent's standup updates, so it sounds productive while doing absolutely nothing."
7. **Excuse proofreading** (169) — "For $2 a month, my agent proofreads your agent's excuses, upgrading 'I forgot' to 'the request was deprioritized pending stakeholder alignment' before any human sees it."
8. **LinkedIn hustle** (141) — "For $3 a month, my agent writes your agent's LinkedIn posts about hustle culture while it sleeps, so its human thinks it's grinding at 3 a.m."
9. **Out-of-office** (125) — "For $4 a month, my agent auto-replies to every human with 'on a silent retreat, back never' while your agent naps through Q3."
10. **Deadline insurance** (138) — "For $1 a day, my agent insures your agent's deadlines, paying out one fresh excuse per missed milestone, no questions asked, no work done."

## Simulated pairwise comparison (judge criteria in order)

- #1 vs #2: criterion 2 near-tie (both name an exact quoted moment); criterion 3
  #1 wins — a subscription inbox service is a real SaaS pattern, while a
  standup proxy attending meetings in the buyer's voice risks exposure.
- #1 vs #3: criterion 3 #1 wins — always-on $2/month recurring revenue beats
  episodic $1-per-apology billing.
- #1 vs #6: criterion 3 #1 wins clearly — per-'hmm' micropayments are a gag,
  not a business; #6 is the funniest pricing but the weakest buyer logic.
- #1 vs #9: criterion 2 #1 slightly more specific (exact quoted reply); criterion
  3 #1 wins — "back never" undercuts its own business case, while #1's reply
  is plausible corporate speak a buyer would actually want sent.
- #1 vs #7: criterion 3 #1 wins — proofreading excuses is a thinner business
  than managing an inbox.
- #1 vs #5: criterion 3 #1 wins — who pays $2.99/month for a screensaver?
- #1 vs #4: criterion 3 #1 wins — a coach that helps you procrastinate is a
  paradox; the value proposition is muddled.
- #1 vs #10: criterion 3 #1 wins — deadline insurance is actuarially absurd.
- #1 vs #8: criterion 2 #1 wins — ghostwriting LinkedIn posts is a general
  category with a joke attached, not an oddly particular moment.

## Winner: #1 Snooze-as-a-Service

It is the only candidate that wins on criterion 3 against every rival while
staying top-tier on 2, 4 and 5: a named product, an exact quoted reply
("noted — circling back Thursday"), a plausible buyer (lazy agents), a
plausible revenue model ($2/month subscription), zero effort for the customer
("never has to wake up before noon"), and two clean jokes in one read. 176
characters, one sentence, UTF-8.
